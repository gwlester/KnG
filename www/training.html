    Installing the apps on Windows

    Everyone · about 1:45

    Use it when you have downloaded an app and need to install it on Windows.

    Download an app, run the installer, and find it in the Start menu.

    Transcript

    This video shows how to install a VCM app on a Windows computer. Your screens may look a little different from these. The steps are the same. In this video: installing the apps on Windows.

    There are four apps for your computer: VCM Administrator, VCM Templates, VCM Services and VCM Runner. You install each one the same way, and you only need the ones for your job.

    Open the download link from your church's technical contact, or go to the Download page on our website. Choose the app you need, then choose Windows. You'll get one installer file for each app.

    When the download finishes, find the file in your Downloads folder and double-click it. Some apps come as an installer with the ending dot E X E, and some with the ending dot M S I. Either one works.

    Windows may warn you that it doesn't recognize the app. That's expected for now. Choose the option to see more details, and then the option to run it anyway.

    Then follow the installer. Choose the button that continues, and again, until it finishes. You don't need to change any of the settings.

    When it's done, open the Start menu and type the name of the app, for example, V C M Administrator. Choose it from the list, and it opens.

    To install another app, repeat these steps with that app's file. You only do this once for each app on each computer.

    That's installing the apps on Windows. Next, watch 'Connecting to your church's Server'.

    Transcript

    This video shows how to install a VCM app on a Mac. Your screens may look a little different from these. The steps are the same. In this video: installing the apps on macOS.

    Installing the apps on Linux

    Everyone · about 1:30

    Use it when you have downloaded an app and need to install it on Linux.

    Install the package with one command and find the app in your applications menu.

    Transcript

    This video shows how to install a VCM app on a Linux computer. Your screens may look a little different from these. The steps are the same. In this video: installing the apps on Linux.

    There are four apps for your computer: VCM Administrator, VCM Templates, VCM Services and VCM Runner. You install each one the same way, and you only need the ones for your job.

    Your computer asks for your password. Type it, and press Enter. You won't see the letters as you type. The installer then does the rest.

    When it's done, the app is in your applications menu, with its own icon, like any other program. Search for its name, for example, V C M Administrator, and open it.

    Connecting to your church's Server

    Start here, for everyone · about 1:55

    Use it when a device needs to connect to your church's VCM Server.

    Find the Server, request enrollment, and get approved. The same steps for every app.

    Transcript

    This is the video everyone needs first. In this video: connecting to your church's Server.

    Everything in Virtual Church Musician lives on one computer at your church: the VCM Server. Every app talks to it over your church's network. All you need is to be on the same Wi-Fi.

    When you open any app, it looks for the Server by itself. This is called Discover. You'll see a progress bar while it searches.

    If your church has more than one Server, for example on two campuses, the app asks which to use. It remembers your choice.

    The very first time, a brand-new Server has no administrator yet. If you see a message asking you to finish setup from VCM Administrator, that's what it means. The administrator does that step; it's covered in the administrator videos.

    Everyone else sees Enroll This Device. Type your username, add a label such as 'Lobby tablet' or 'Office PC' so the administrator can tell devices apart, and choose Request Enrollment.

    A Waiting for Approval window appears. Someone with VCM Administrator has to approve you. You can leave this window open, or choose Check Now once you know it's done.

    Once you're approved, you'll be asked to choose a password and agree to the license.

    Choose a password, agree to the license, and you're in.

    If the app can't find the Server: check you're on the same Wi-Fi, try Discover a second time, and if it still can't, the Server may be off. Ask your technical contact.

    That's connecting to your church's Server, and everyone needs this one. Next, choose the videos for your job on the Training page: administrators, service planners and whoever presses Play each have their own.

    Your password, locking the app, and saved servers

    Everyone · about 1:15

    Use it when you want a new password, you are stepping away, or the wrong Server got selected.

    Change your password, lock the app, and see or forget the Servers this device knows.

    Transcript

    This video assumes your device is already connected. If it isn't, watch 'Connecting to your church's Server' first. In this video: your password, locking the app, and saved servers.

    To change your password, open the File menu and choose Change Password. Every app has it.

    Type your current password, then your new one twice, and confirm. Use something you'll remember, and keep it to yourself.

    To step away, lock the app with Control-L, or Command-L on a Mac. It asks for your password when you come back. Most apps also lock themselves after sitting idle.

    Type your password to unlock. VCM Runner is the exception: it never locks itself, so it stays ready through a whole service.

    Under File, Saved Servers, you can see every Server this device has enrolled with, and forget the ones you no longer need.

    If the wrong Server got selected, forget it here. The next time you connect, the app asks you to choose again.

    That's your password, locking the app, and saved servers. Now choose the videos for your job on the Training page.

    Quick start: approving and rejecting enrollments

    For administrators · about 1:30

    Use it when someone asks to connect a device.

    Approve or reject a device's request to connect. The most common administrator task.

    Transcript

    This video assumes your device is already connected. If it isn't, watch 'Connecting to your church's Server' first. In this video: approving and rejecting enrollments, the most common administrator task.

    Your church's Server only lets in devices you approve, and this is the task you'll do most often. Open VCM Administrator, choose the Security tab, then Pending Enrollments.

    Each row is a device asking to connect. You can see who asked, which app it is, and the label they gave the device, such as Office PC or Lobby tablet. Make sure you recognize the request before you go on.

    If it's someone you expect, select the row and choose Approve. The device is let in, and the person is asked to choose a password and agree to the license.

    If you don't recognize a request, select it and choose Reject. You'll be asked for a reason. The person sees your reason, and they're welcome to try again.

    Handled requests leave this list. To see everyone you've approved, or to remove a device later, look under Devices. That's covered in the video on managing devices and users.

    That's approving and rejecting enrollments. To manage devices you've already approved, watch 'Managing devices and users'. If you approve devices from your phone, watch 'Approving a device from your phone'.

    Managing devices and users

    For administrators · about 1:25

    Use it when a device is lost, or someone leaves.

    Revoke, suspend, resume and sign out devices; suspend a person.

    Transcript

    This video assumes your device is already connected. If it isn't, watch 'Connecting to your church's Server' first. In this video: managing devices and users.

    Open the Security tab and choose Devices. This lists every device that has ever been approved, with its app, its label and its status.

    To block a device for a while, select it and choose Suspend. The device can't connect until you say so. Use this when you're not sure yet what happened.

    To let it back in, select it and choose Resume. Nothing is lost.

    Sign Out ends the device's current session without taking away its access. It simply has to log in again next time.

    For a device that is lost or stolen, choose Revoke. That takes its access away for good, and the device has to enroll from the beginning. You'll be asked to confirm, so it's safe to look.

    The Users tab lists everyone with an account. Choose Suspend here to block a person on every one of their devices at once, and Resume to let them back. Add User creates a new account.

    That's managing devices and users. To approve new devices, watch 'Quick start: approving and rejecting enrollments'. To find out whether everything is healthy, watch 'Checking that everything is healthy'.

    Checking that everything is healthy

    For administrators · about 1:00

    Use it when before a service, or after a change.

    Read the Dashboard and Connection tabs.

    Transcript

    This video assumes your device is already connected. If it isn't, watch 'Connecting to your church's Server' first. In this video: checking that everything is healthy.

    Open the Dashboard tab. It shows how much content your Server holds: hymnals, hymns, chants, seasons, categories and templates. If those numbers look right, your music is there.

    The Diagnostics tab shows which Server you're connected to, its status, and how many warnings and errors it has reported. You want to see Connected, with zero warnings and zero errors.

    The Connection tab shows the Server's address and port, and below it, a log of what has happened recently. Check that the address is the one you expect.

    If you've changed something, choose Refresh at the top to bring the numbers up to date.

    That's checking that everything is healthy. If something isn't, watch 'When something goes wrong'.

    VCM Server settings

    For administrators · about 1:00

    Use it when you are changing how the VCM Server behaves.

    Site name, sound or MIDI device, MIDI or MP3 preference, and the idle lock time.

    Transcript

    This video assumes your device is already connected. If it isn't, watch 'Connecting to your church's Server' first. In this video: VCM Server settings.

    Open the Settings tab. The top part shows how this app connects to the Server. The part below, Server Configuration, is the Server's own settings, and it applies to the whole church.

    Here you can set the site or campus name, the sound output device, the MIDI device, and whether the Server prefers to play M I D I or M P 3 when both are available.

    When something goes wrong

    For administrators · about 1:00

    Use it when something is not working.

    Diagnostics, application log levels and fetching the server log, to share with your technical contact.

    Transcript

    This video assumes your device is already connected. If it isn't, watch 'Connecting to your church's Server' first. In this video: what to do when something goes wrong.

    Start with the Diagnostics tab. It shows the Server's status and how many warnings and errors it has reported. Anything above zero is worth telling your technical contact about.

    The Snapshot button saves a diagnostic report to a file. You can send that file along to your technical contact. It's also under the File menu.

    On the Connection tab, choose Fetch Server Log to load the Server's own log of what it has been doing. The Filter button narrows it down.

    Your technical contact may ask you to raise the logging detail. That's under the Security tab, in Application Log Levels. Only change it when you're asked to.

    That's what to do when something goes wrong. Send what you gathered to your technical contact.

    Service template designer

    Designing a service template with VCM Templates

    For service template designers · about 1:45

    Use it when you are building or changing a template.

    Create, edit, clone and version a reusable starting point for your services.

    Transcript

    This video assumes your device is already connected. If it isn't, watch 'Connecting to your church's Server' first. In this video: designing a service template with VCM Templates.

    A template is a reusable starting point for a service. It holds the order of the service, and if you like, the actual hymns and chants. Then VCM Services can build a new service from it, instead of starting from scratch each week.

    Choose Create to start a new template. Give it a name, and if you like, a description and a category.

    Choose Add to put in an item. Type its name, choose whether it's a hymn or a chant, and choose Save. Do that for each item, in the order they'll be sung.

    Select an item to move it with Up and Down, change it with Edit, or take it out with Remove.

    When the order looks right, choose Save. Every save here goes straight to the Server. There's no local draft to lose.

    To start a new template from an existing one without changing the original, select it and choose Clone. That's ideal for a service that's almost, but not quite, the same.

    Every save creates a new version, and the older ones are kept. Rebase renumbers the newest one back to version 1 once you don't need the old ones. And on a computer, you can Export a template to a file, or Import one someone shared with you.

    That's designing a service template. To plan a service from a template, watch 'Planning a service from a template with VCM Services'.

    Planning a service from a template with VCM Services

    For service planners · about 1:45

    Use it when you are planning a service.

    Pick a template and a date, plug in this week's hymns, fix any red items, and publish without losing your work.

    Transcript

    This video assumes your device is already connected. If it isn't, watch 'Connecting to your church's Server' first. In this video: planning a service from a template with VCM Services.

    VCM Services is where you put together the actual order of service for a specific date: the hymns, chants and other items, in order.

    Choose Create, and pick a template to start from. That saves time, because the order of the service is already there. Then choose Select.

    The Publish button stays off until every red item is fixed. When the last one is done, it comes on.

    Save Local keeps your work on this device only, and an unpublished draft is lost when the app closes. So when you're ready, and now that we have addressed all of the issues, we press Publish to save our work on the Server and make it available to others, including VCM Runner.

    Running a service with VCM Runner

    For whoever presses Play · about 1:40

    Use it when you run the music during a service.

    Load today's service and press Play, and what to do if the connection drops.

    Transcript

    This video assumes your device is already connected. If it isn't, watch 'Connecting to your church's Server' first. In this video: running a service with VCM Runner.

    VCM Runner is the app you use during a live service. It's deliberately simple: find today's service, which the app calls a script, and press Play.

    When it opens, choose the hymnal, the date and, if you have more than one service that day, the time. Then choose Continue. If exactly one published service matches, it loads by itself.

    Each item in the service is a row. It's pending, next to play, playing, or already played.

    Select an item and press Play to start it, or Stop to end it early.

    Items set to auto-advance move on by themselves. Others wait for you to start the next one.

    If the connection drops, choose Reconnect. You'll pick up where you were, without choosing the service again.

    Only one VCM Runner, on a computer or a phone, should be in control of playback at a time. And this app never locks itself when idle, so it's ready right through a sermon. If the music doesn't play, or plays through the wrong device, tell your technical contact which item it was.

    That's running a service with VCM Runner. If you also plan services, watch 'Planning a service from a template with VCM Services'.
