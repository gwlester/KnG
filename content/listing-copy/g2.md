## Product name

Virtual Church Musician (VCM)

## Category

Church Management Software (closest existing G2 category; note in the
profile that VCM specifically covers service planning and live music
playback, not the donation/membership/scheduling side of that category)

## Short description

Software for planning a church's music and running it live -- MP3 or
MIDI accompaniment cued automatically, so the service doesn't depend on
someone operating playback by hand.

## Full description

Virtual Church Musician gives music and worship staff a Templates app for
reusable service shapes, a Services app to build a specific week's order
from hymns and chants, and a Runner app that plays that order live --
stereo MP3 audio or MIDI sent directly to an instrument, or through a
dedicated MIDI Player, cued automatically at the right point in the
service. An Administrator app and a mobile Security companion manage
device and user enrollment. Everything connects to a Server that runs
fully air-gapped on the church's own network by design -- no service or
attendance data is ever sent to the vendor.

Desktop apps run on Windows, macOS, and Linux; mobile apps run on Android.
The interface is available in English, Spanish, and German.

## Platforms supported

Windows, macOS, Linux, Android

## Similar tools

In the same general category: **HymnSoft**, **Christian Worship: Service
Builder** (with its **Playlist** add-on), and **MyMIDI Worship
Resources**. The clearest capability difference from Service Builder +
Playlist: Playlist plays back encoded audio recordings (MP3/M4A) instead
of MIDI, by its publisher's own stated design; VCM sends MIDI directly to
a real instrument during the live service.

## Pricing

Freemium -- client apps (Admin/Security, Templates, Services, Runner) are
free; the Server and MIDI Player are commercially licensed per church.
Sales channel and pricing not yet public as of this writing.

## Best for

Churches and ministries that want live music/accompaniment handled
reliably without a dedicated operator at the board every week --
particularly smaller churches and volunteer-run tech teams.
